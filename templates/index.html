What Our AI Detects

A clinical overview of the three tumor types our model is trained to identify.

MALIGNANT — GRADE I–IV

Glioma

Originating from glial cells that support and protect neurons, Gliomas are the most common primary brain tumors. They exhibit infiltrative growth and range from slow-growing Grade I tumors to Grade IV Glioblastomas, which are among the most aggressive cancers known to medicine.

Prevalence

Accounts for ~33% of all brain tumors

Common Symptoms

Headaches, seizures, cognitive changes

Treatment

Surgery, radiation therapy, chemotherapy

TYPICALLY BENIGN

Meningioma

Arising from the meninges — the protective membranes surrounding the brain — Meningiomas are typically slow-growing and most often benign. However, due to their location, they can exert critical pressure on brain structures and spinal cord, causing significant neurological deficits.

Prevalence

Most frequent CNS tumor — ~37% of cases

Common Symptoms

Often silent; headaches, vision changes

Treatment

Observation, surgery, or radiosurgery

MOSTLY NON-CANCEROUS ADENOMA

Pituitary Tumor

Located at the base of the brain within the sella turcica, these tumors develop in the pituitary gland — the master regulator of hormones. While mostly benign adenomas, they can disrupt hormonal balance, compress the optic chiasm, and cause systemic metabolic effects throughout the body.

Prevalence

~16% of all intracranial neoplasms

Common Symptoms

Hormonal imbalance, vision loss, fatigue

Treatment

Medication, transsphenoidal surgery
